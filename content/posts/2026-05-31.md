---
title: "Software is having its "Industrial Revolution" moment"
description: "A reflection on the fairly obvious state of the industry"
date: "2026-05-31"
---

Writing software has been closer to castle building than model-T production historically. 
In the last 30 years or so we've build the infrastructure needed to fully industrialize.
We created standardized parts (i.e. packages), unit/end-to-end/ui testing, and CI/CD pipelines to utilize them.
LLM/AI agents and coding harnesses are the last part of the basic building blocks needed to create something akin to the modern manufacturing process.

Like the modern manufacturing process, we are handing off our specs to third party factories to build. Its up to the company looking for manufacturing partners to
validate the prospective factories and build in effective testing of their ouput. That's where software engineering
is at right now. Building effective, efficient methods to hand off specs to AI agents and test their output.

There's a window right now where orgs with the resources to do so can spend time building systems to reduce the amount human input and review per task.
On a long enough timeline, solutions will trickle down as open source solutions are built and best practices align around how to effectively use agents.
For now, the orgs that can build solitions, should.

Start by looking at what parts of the process are eating your engineers' time and start building tooling around automating any unautomated dependencies.
Once all the dependencies are automated, automate that step. Once that is done, move one step right and repeat until automation nonlonger reduces engineer input.
Repeat for other processes.
